The Long Game

Preparation: Trusting the Process (Mostly)

Every certification journey starts with that beautiful, delusional confidence. You crack open Tutorial Dojo, start hammering mock exams, get a decent score, and think: yeah, I've got this.

I was one of those people. I practiced religiously. I went through mock after mock. The scenarios in Tutorial Dojo felt familiar, the logic was clicking. By the time I hit my exam date, I felt prepared — the kind of prepared that comes with a slight swagger and a "let's get this done" energy.

Real Talk

Only about 10% of the actual exam questions felt remotely similar to Tutorial Dojo mocks. The other 90%? Brand new scenarios. AWS is not here to repeat itself.

But here's the thing — that doesn't mean the preparation was wasted. Far from it. What the mocks actually trained was thinking, not memorising. The scenarios changed but the mental framework — read carefully, identify what AWS is really asking, eliminate aggressively — that transferred perfectly.

Two things made a real difference in prep:

🃏
Flashcards on Tutorial Dojo AWS SAA is fundamentally an exam about knowing AWS services — what they do, when to use them, what makes them different. The flashcards drill exactly this. They're not glamorous. They work.
📓
Personal notes — a greatest hits of failure Every question I got wrong in Tutorial Dojo, I noted it down. The night before the exam, I revised nothing else. Just those notes. Just the gaps. That last-day revision hit different.

Think of the flashcards as your product catalogue — AWS is essentially advertising its own services in every question, and you need to know the catalogue well. Know the product, know the answer.

Exam Day: Documents, Signatures & Mild Anxiety

I booked an offline exam centre — a Sunday at 10:30 AM. On paper, a relaxed start. In practice, I was at the venue by 9:45 AM, because apparently "be 15 minutes early" is hardwired into my entire personality.

Here's the check-in process if you've never done a Pearson VUE offline exam before:

Starting 20 minutes early was a nice surprise. The room was quiet. The kind of quiet that makes you suddenly hyper-aware of your own breathing. And then — the first question appeared on screen.

Inside the Exam

What AWS Actually Tests (Spoiler: Scenarios. All Scenarios.)

Let me set expectations correctly for anyone who thinks this is a straightforward "what does S3 stand for" kind of exam. It is not. Not even a little bit.

Every. Single. Question. Was a scenario. Some company, some problem, some constraint — and you have to architect a solution. There were zero direct factual questions. AWS doesn't care if you can recite the definition of a NAT Gateway. It wants to know if you'd use one over a NAT instance in a multi-AZ setup for a cost-sensitive workload.

The multi-answer questions — 7 to 8 with two correct picks, 4 to 5 with three — are where people trip. You can't just find one right answer and move on. You have to hold multiple correct ideas in your head simultaneously, which means half-right answers will absolutely get you if you're rushing.

The Real Secret Weapon

Read the question fully. Then read it again. Then read the last line one more time. The question tells you exactly what to optimise for — cost, performance, security, availability. Once you know the constraint, eliminate ruthlessly.

The difficulty felt like a solid medium — not a bloodbath, but definitely not easy. Questions were carefully worded, options were plausible enough that overconfidence will betray you. The exam rewards people who think, not people who memorise.

What actually saved me was slowing down. Reading every option. Not picking the first thing that sounded familiar but asking: "Does this actually solve what the scenario is asking?" Elimination was my best friend. Knock out the two obviously wrong options, then choose carefully from what remains.

🔍
Find the priority word first The question almost always tells you the priority — "cost-effective", "highly available", "lowest operational overhead". Whatever word appears there, that's your north star for the entire question.
📣
AWS is advertising its own services Every question is an opportunity to say "and this is why you should use AWS Service X". Know the services, know their strengths, and you're already ahead of 70% of the room.

"The scenarios will surprise you. The specific questions will be nothing like your mocks. And somehow — if you've built the right mental model — you'll know what to do anyway."

Passing the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate wasn't about being brilliant. It was about being prepared in the right way — flashcards for service knowledge, failed questions for gap-filling, and genuine slow careful reading during the exam itself.

That's the whole point of the exam. And now you know it too.
